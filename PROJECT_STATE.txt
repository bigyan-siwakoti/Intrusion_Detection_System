CAVEAT — TOPOLOGY RULES. Classified every rule by its text (docs/results/round_rule_classes.json):
  topology = ordinary protocol activity from outside HOME_NET with no attack constraint
  (SMTP MAIL FROM, POP3/FTP login success, SIP INVITE/REGISTER, POP3 RETR, PROPFIND, ...).
  In UNSW every external host is an attacker, so these score perfectly and would fail on a real
  network. Plain R2: 10 topology, 3 behavioural, 1 signature, 2 dataset-specific strings
  (www.exa.dpn, dc=foobar,dc=com). Research R2: 5 topology, 5 behavioural, 2 signature.
  Re-scored R2:            all rules   no topology   behavioural+signature only
    plain ...............   36.3%        28.3%            25.0%  (+0.1)
    research ............   27.3%        24.9%            24.9%  (+0.0)
  → On the strong baseline, the AI's genuine rules add ~nothing; the "gain" is topology rules
    and generator-specific strings. Its genuine rules mostly rediscover what the re-enabled
    human families already cover (they did add ~+6 pts over ET Open alone in v1).
  Also topology: CIC 7600102/7600103 ("Heartbleed recon" = any outside host making 5 TLS
    connections in 5 min) — the report's "CIC rules transfer to UNSW" claim rests on these.
  Cross-check on CIC Thursday: all 28 round rules silent, but INCONCLUSIVE — CIC has almost no
    benign inbound service traffic (Thu: 1 SMTP, 1 POP3, 1 LDAP inbound flows vs ~130k outbound).
    Neither dataset can test topology rules; state as a limitation.
